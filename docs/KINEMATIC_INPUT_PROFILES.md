# Humanized Kinematic Input Profiles

**Date**: 2026-11-18  
**Status**: Design reference, partly implemented; see "Audit notes" at the end (audited 2026-11-19)  
**Workstream**: `W101-NAVIGATION` / `CORE-DELIVERY`  
**Reference Document**: Mathematical formulation, biomechanical modeling, and runtime contract for non-robotic input dispatch.

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Biomechanical Motivation

Synthetic input generated by naive automation frameworks typically exhibits robotic artifacts:
1. **Instantaneous Teleportation**: Moving the cursor directly to target coordinates $(x_t, y_t)$ in a single hardware frame ($\Delta t \approx 0$).
2. **Linear Trajectories with Constant Velocity**: Discretizing movement into straight-line increments ($\frac{dx}{dt} = \text{const}$), producing unnatural zero-curvature paths with instantaneous acceleration and deceleration (infinite jerk: $\frac{d^3x}{dt^3} \to \infty$).
3. **Isochronous Temporal Cadence**: Fixed delay timers (e.g. `sleep(100ms)`) between key down and key up, or perfectly uniform inter-keystroke intervals.

Human motor execution, governed by the central nervous system and neuromuscular mechanics, obeys well-established physical and empirical laws:
- **Minimum Jerk Theory**: Human voluntary movements maximize smoothness by minimizing the time integral of squared jerk ($\int_0^T \|\dddot{\mathbf{x}}(t)\|^2 dt$).
- **Fitts's Law**: Target acquisition time is a logarithmic function of distance divided by target width.
- **Sub-movement Corrections**: Approaching a target involves ballistic coarse movement followed by sensory feedback-driven corrective sub-movements and slight overshooting.
- **Log-Normal Temporal Distributions**: Human reaction and dwell times follow asymmetric log-normal probability distributions with sharp onset and extended positive tails, rather than uniform or Gaussian distributions.

Maelstrom integrates native, zero-dependency kinematic generators in `crates/maelstrom-core/src/kinematics.rs` to compute realistic mouse trajectories and keystroke durations.

## 2. Mathematical Models & Algorithms

### 2.1 WindMouse Physics Simulation

The WindMouse algorithm models the mouse cursor as a particle of virtual mass subjected to a gravitational force pulling toward the target, an opposing damping resistance (viscous friction), and a stochastic wind force modeling involuntary neuromuscular tremor and drift.

Let $\mathbf{x}_k = (x_k, y_k)$ denote cursor position, $\mathbf{v}_k = (v_{x,k}, v_{y,k})$ denote velocity, and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{target}}$ denote the target point.

#### Force Components:
1. **Gravitational Attraction**:
   $$\mathbf{F}_{\text{grav}} = G \cdot \frac{\mathbf{x}_{\text{target}} - \mathbf{x}_k}{\|\mathbf{x}_{\text{target}} - \mathbf{x}_k\|}$$
   where $G$ is the gravitational pull parameter.
2. **Stochastic Wind (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Process)**:
   $$\mathbf{v}_{\text{wind}, k+1} = \frac{\mathbf{v}_{\text{wind}, k}}{\sqrt{3}} + \frac{\mathcal{U}(-W, W)}{\sqrt{5}}$$
   where $W$ is the maximum wind force magnitude, generating autoregressive, continuous drift rather than uncorrelated white noise.
3. **Dynamic Damping & Deceleration**:
   When distance $d = \|\mathbf{x}_{\text{target}} - \mathbf{x}_k\| \le D_{\text{threshold}}$:
   - Wind force is scaled down linearly with distance: $W_{\text{eff}} = W \cdot \frac{d}{D_{\text{threshold}}}$ (`eff_wind = wind * d / threshold` in the code).
   - Gravity is scaled by $\max(d / D_{\text{threshold}}, 0.4)$, so the cursor slows near the target. There is no separate damping coefficient: the speed clamp $v_{\max}$ is the only velocity limit, the loop ends within 2.5 px of the target, and a final step snaps to it. Micro-overshoot is neither produced by design nor tested.

#### Discrete State Update:
$$\mathbf{v}_{k+1} = \mathbf{v}_k + \mathbf{F}_{\text{grav}} + \mathbf{v}_{\text{wind}, k+1}$$
$$\mathbf{v}_{k+1} = \text{clamp}(\mathbf{v}_{k+1}, v_{\text{max}})$$
$$\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{v}_{k+1}$$

### 2.2 Cubic Bézier Curves with Asymmetric Minimum-Jerk Velocity Profiles

For rapid UI interactions (e.g. clicking spell cards in hand, selecting potions, clicking quest dialog buttons), a parameterized cubic Bézier trajectory with dynamic control point displacement provides smooth curvature:

$$\mathbf{B}(t) = (1-t)^3 \mathbf{P}_0 + 3(1-t)^2 t \mathbf{P}_1 + 3(1-t) t^2 \mathbf{P}_2 + t^3 \mathbf{P}_3, \quad t \in [0, 1]$$

#### Control Point Geometry:
- $\mathbf{P}_0$: Starting cursor position.
- $\mathbf{P}_3$: Random target point sampled within target UI bounding box (never the exact mathematical center).
- $\mathbf{P}_1$: Departure point perturbed along initial velocity vector with orthogonal deviation $\delta_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_1)$.
- $\mathbf{P}_2$: Arrival point positioned along target approach vector with orthogonal deviation $\delta_2 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_2)$.

#### Asymmetric Bell-Shaped Velocity Profile:
Rather than uniform time steps $t_k = k/N$, parameter $t$ is evaluated via a 5th-order polynomial (minimum jerk trajectory):
$$s(\tau) = 10\tau^3 - 15\tau^4 + 6\tau^5, \quad \tau = \frac{t_{\text{elapsed}}}{T_{\text{total}}} \in [0, 1]$$
- Acceleration phase: $0 \le \tau \le 0.35$ (rapid peak velocity).
- Deceleration phase: $0.35 < \tau \le 1.0$ (prolonged braking with terminal settling).

---

### 2.3 Fitts's Law Target Acquisition Duration

The total movement duration $T_{\text{total}}$ for acquiring a UI target of bounding width $W_t$ located at distance $D_t = \|\mathbf{P}_3 - \mathbf{P}_0\|$ is governed by Fitts's Law (Shannon formulation):

$$T_{\text{total}} = a + b \cdot \log_2\left(1 + \frac{D_t}{W_t}\right)$$

#### Empirical Baseline Calibration:
- Intercept parameter $a \approx 120\text{ms}$ (minimum neuromuscular initiation and ballistic launch time).
- Slope parameter $b \approx 160\text{ms}$ (information processing rate $\approx 6.25 \text{ bits/s}$).
- For a small card ($W_t = 40\text{px}$) across the screen ($D_t = 800\text{px}$), index of difficulty $\text{ID} = \log_2(1 + 20) \approx 4.39$, yielding $T_{\text{total}} \approx 822\text{ms}$.
- For a close button ($D_t = 80\text{px}$, $W_t = 60\text{px}$), $\text{ID} \approx 1.22$, yielding $T_{\text{total}} \approx 315\text{ms}$.

---

### 2.4 Log-Normal Temporal Jitter & Dwell Times

Human button presses and keyboard strokes exhibit variable dwell durations (time between `KEY_DOWN` / `MOUSE_DOWN` and `KEY_UP` / `MOUSE_UP`). The probability density function of motor dwell time is log-normal:

$$f(t; \mu, \sigma) = \frac{1}{t \sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left(-\frac{(\ln t - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$

#### Calibration Constants:
- **Mouse Click Dwell**:
  - $\mu = \ln(75) \approx 4.317$, $\sigma = 0.22$.
  - Median duration $\approx 75\text{ms}$; the central 95% interval of this distribution is about $[49\text{ms}, 115\text{ms}]$ before the code's clamp to 40-180 ms.
- **Steering Key Pulse Dwell (`W`, `A`, `D`)**:
  - Controlled by Pure Pursuit curvature and cadence in `steering.rs`, with log-normal edge jitter $\pm 12\text{ms}$.
- **Inter-Keystroke Interval (Cadence)**:
  - $\mu = \ln(130) \approx 4.867$, $\sigma = 0.28$.
  - Median cadence $\approx 130\text{ms}$, suppressing mechanical periodicity.

```
│  │           Kinematics Trajectory Planner          │  │
│  │   - WindMouse Simulator                          │  │
│  │   - Cubic Bézier + Minimum-Jerk Timings          │  │
│  │   - Fitts's Law Acquisition Durations            │  │
│  │   - Log-Normal Temporal Sampling                 │  │
│  └──────────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘  │
│                             │                          │
│                             ▼                          │
│     KinematicPlan (Timed Points: (x, y, dt_ms))        │
│                             │                          │
└─────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┘
                              │ Facts ≠ Authority ≠ Actions
                              ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             Platform Layer / Host Actuator             │
│   (Checks Input Lease, Active Focus, OS Boundaries)    │
│   (Dispatches timed SendInput / Hardware events)       │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Architectural Invariants:
1. **`Facts ≠ Authority ≠ Actions`**:
   - `kinematics.rs` is a pure mathematical calculation engine. It does not import OS input APIs, does not mutate external hardware state, and does not dispatch commands.
   - It outputs immutable trajectory plans (`KinematicPlan`) consisting of timestamped coordinate waypoints and action durations.
2. **Determinism with Seeded Pseudo-Randomness**:
   - Random drift and perturbations utilize an internal deterministic linear congruential generator (LCG) or Xorshift PRNG when seeded, allowing identical replay in offline tests and simulation.
3. **Zero Heap Allocations in Hot Loops**:
   - Uses fixed-capacity point arrays or pre-allocated vectors.

## Audit notes (2026-11-19)
Checked against `crates/maelstrom-core/src/kinematics.rs` and `steering.rs`; the text above is kept.
- **Implemented as described:** WindMouse generator with Ornstein-Uhlenbeck-style wind (1/sqrt3 and 1/sqrt5 factors) and proximity damping; XorShift64* seeded PRNG; cubic Bezier trajectory using the 10t^3 - 15t^4 + 6t^5 timing polynomial; Fitts model with a = 120 ms and b = 160 ms (the worked examples, about 822 ms and 315 ms, check out arithmetically); log-normal dwell profiles (click mu 4.317, sigma 0.22; cadence mu 4.867, sigma 0.28). The 7 `kinematics` unit tests pass.
- **Not as described:**
  - The "asymmetric" profile (acceleration to tau = 0.35, then prolonged braking) is not in the code. The minimum-jerk polynomial is symmetric and no 0.35 split exists.
  - Bezier control points sit at fixed 0.33 and 0.67 along the chord with Gaussian lateral offsets, not along an initial velocity vector. Target points come from a separate `sample_target_in_rect` (Gaussian clustering around the centre), not uniform sampling in the box.
  - "Zero heap allocations in hot loops / fixed-capacity arrays": paths use `Vec::with_capacity`, one allocation per generated path.
  - The "+/-12 ms log-normal edge jitter" for `W`/`A`/`D` pulses is not in `steering.rs`; no jitter or log-normal sampling was found there.
- **"Empirical Baseline Calibration" is overstated.** The Fitts and dwell constants are generic starting values, not measured from human or game data. The quoted percentile range for clicks (48-120 ms) is approximate; the code clamps dwell to 40-180 ms.
- **Wiring:** the generated plans are consumed by the C# actuator only as a summary and the reviewed live/simulation paths use stubbed delegates (see the kinematics audit in `W101-KINEMATICS-CORE-02`). Nothing here is evidence of any live game outcome.

- **Second audit (2026-11-19, pass 33)**, against `maelstrom_bridge.rs` and `DesktopInputActuator.cs`. The result file
  `W101-KINEMATICS-DOCS-01` holds the evidence.
  - Sections 2.1 and 2.4 had wrong wording ("exponentially suppressed", "damping coefficient increases", "95th percentile
    [48 ms, 120 ms]"); they are fixed in place. The first bullet above that calls the WindMouse generator "implemented as
    described" should be read with that correction.
  - **The curved, timed path in section 3 is generated and then discarded.** The summary sent to C# carries point count, total
